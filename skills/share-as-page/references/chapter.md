# Chapters that teach

A chapter is the second page shape, beside the brief. A brief lets one reader act in seconds. A chapter lets one reader learn one subject in an hour and trust every sentence. The reader is an engineering leader. The subject is one dimension of a platform. The chapter is 3,000 to 8,000 words, one self-contained HTML file, and it prints.

Ruled 5 Nov 2026 after a deep research pass (agent 360cebe8, `2026-11-05-textbook-chapter-type`). The rules below are what the evidence supports. Each rule names its evidence so a later pass can retire it.

## The shape, in order

Every chapter has these parts, in this order, and nothing is collapsed.

| # | Part | What it is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kicker | Series, chapter number, brand, date, word count. | House. |
| 2 | Title | The chapter's thesis as one sentence under ten words. The topic is in the kicker, not the title. | Message titles are what readers recall (Borkin 2016: titles described in 46% of recalls; message titles recalled the message, generic ones did not). |
| 3 | Lede | Under 60 words. First sentence in bold answers the title. | House brief rule. |
| 4 | What you will be able to do | Three to five statements, each specific to a section below. Not questions. | Pre-statements work as well as pre-questions (Rivers 2026). The effect is specific: g = 0.54 on the items named, g = 0.04 on the rest (St. Hilaire 2024, k = 97). A vague objective buys nothing. |
| 5 | Terms you need | The components named before they are used. One line each. | Pre-training: median d = 1.00 over three studies (Mayer & Moreno 2003). |
| 6 | Contents | The section headings as links, not sticky. | A TOC raises discovery of content low on the page. In the body it should not be sticky (NN/g 2023). |
| 7 | Sections | Numbered. Each heading is a sentence with a verb. Each section ends with one line that says what it showed. Paragraphs under 80 words. | Signaling d = 0.46, segmenting d = 0.70 (Mayer 2017). Headings as claims: the argument survives when only the headings are read (assertion-evidence, Alley). Frequent in-text summaries raised recall, d = 1.57 (van der Meij 2012, one study). |
| 8 | Figures | Every figure has a headline that states the finding and a subtitle that names the measure, the place and the period. A source line and a data table sit under it. | Two-title rule (UK Analysis Function). Source under every chart (Our World in Data). Values readable without colour (WCAG 1.4.11). |
| 9 | Worked example | One per chapter, on the real platform, labelled so an expert can skip it. Never collapsed. | Worked examples help novices and lose their effect with expertise (Kalyuga et al. 2003). |
| 10 | Check yourself | Three to five questions that need recall or application, not recognition. Answers sit at the end of the page, after the sources. | Testing at one week: 61% recall after repeated testing against 40% after repeated study (Roediger & Karpicke 2006, Exp. 2, d = 1.26). Practice testing is one of two high-utility techniques (Dunlosky 2013). |
| 11 | In one paragraph | The chapter in under 120 words. | Evidence for end summaries is thin. Self-written summaries rate low (Dunlosky 2013). The section tails carry the running summary. This block is a courtesy, not a lever. |
| 12 | Audit of this chapter | A table: claims by verdict, the verifier, the date, and the unchecked count. Generated by `chapter-audit`. | Fabricated citations run 14% to 95% across 13 models (GhostCite 2026); 18% for GPT-4 in Walters & Wilder 2023. An unaudited citation list is not evidence. |
| 13 | Sources | Numbered. Each entry: who, what, when, live URL, archived URL, access date, version or commit, and the supporting quote. | Half of the links in Supreme Court opinions no longer held the cited material (Harvard Law Review 2014). Software needs the version or commit (FORCE11 2016). |
| 14 | Answers | To the questions in part 10. | Retrieval first, then feedback. |

## Citations: one claim, one source that states it

- **Cite at the sentence.** A number after the sentence it supports. A source supports a sentence only if the fact is present explicitly in the source (Wikipedia Verifiability, "directly supports"). If the source implies it, the sentence says "which suggests" and the audit marks it inferred.
- **Every number has a citation.** A count, a size, a date, a price, a percentage. The reference says which table, section or line it came from.
- **Numbered, not author-date.** Distill chose numbers "to improve readability of citation dense articles". The chapter is citation dense.
- **The reference carries the quote.** The sentence from the source that supports the claim, verbatim, under 50 words. The audit checks that the quote is in the source. A quote that is not in the source fails the claim.
- **The reference carries the archive.** Save every web source with the Wayback Machine at writing time and cite both addresses with the access date. A GitHub file is cited at a commit, never at a branch. A release is cited with its version and date. A paper is cited with its DOI or arXiv id and version.
- **Tiers stay in the record, not on the page.** The reference list shows the source. The audit shows the verdict. The tier reasoning lives in the research archive.

## The audit: existence, support, independence

Each claim gets a record with these fields: `id`, `claim`, `ref`, `quote`, `verdict`, `certainty`, `verifier`, `checked`.

- **Verdicts:** supported, partial, inferred, unsupported, contradicted, unreachable, unchecked. These follow Wikipedia's split between no source, dead source, and a source that was read and does not support the claim (Template:Failed verification).
- **Existence is mechanical.** `chapter-audit` fetches each source, or its archive, and checks the quote is in the text. Not found is a fail the author must fix.
- **Support is judged by a different model than the author.** Same-family judges prefer their own output (FACTS Grounding uses three judges from three families for this reason). An LLM verifier agreed with humans on 72% of facts (SAFE 2024), so the table keeps a column for a human spot check, empty until a person fills it.
- **Certainty is a separate field from the verdict.** A claim can be fully supported by a weak source. Four levels, as GRADE uses: high, moderate, low, very low. Likelihood words and confidence words are different things (IPCC guidance).
- **Unchecked claims stay visible.** The audit table counts them, and each carries a hollow marker in the text. Nothing is silently dropped.

## Figures: a mechanism per figure, in one grammar

Ruled 5 Nov 2026 after the first-principles pass (`2026-11-05-textbook-information-design`). The measured exemplars (Crafting Interpreters, OSTEP, the Rust book, Distill) run 0.4 to 1.9 figures per thousand words, and what they draw is state and mechanism, not charts. A chapter needs four to eight such figures, and almost no tables.

- **Every figure carries one mechanism or one state**, and the caption's bold run-in states it as a sentence (Rougier rules 2 and 4). A figure that restates the text gives nothing (Larkin and Simon).
- **A boundary is an enclosing region with no arrow. Layers are rows. An arrow means something moves or causes**, is labelled with what crosses it, and a figure uses at most two arrow styles (Heiser and Tversky 2006).
- **Every box carries a type and a role** (C4 notation). Identity is labelled directly. A key covers notation only.
- **One accent for the thing being taught, one warn tone for the attacker or the break, everything else grey** (Red Blob, Distill). Text inside a figure never wears the data colour. Smallest effective difference.
- **Simplified beats detailed** (Butcher 2006). Five to nine boxes. One flow.
- **Same layout for a comparison**, so the one thing that changes is the only thing that moves (small multiples).
- **Draw with `figlib.py`**, which encodes the grammar as inline SVG. `hq-diagram` stays for flows with a branch, merge or loop when Graphviz layout is wanted.
- **Charts** keep the two-title rule (UK Analysis Function), 14px text (ONS), five colours or fewer, direct labels, a data table under the chart (WCAG 1.4.11) and alt text of the form "[chart type] of [data] showing [finding]".
- **Twenty numbers or fewer is a table** (Tufte), and a table is for lookups only. A table that explains a mechanism is a figure that has not been drawn yet.

## The page, set for an hour of reading

- **Body in the house serif at 19px, line-height 1.6, measure 66 characters** (Bringhurst 2.1.2: 45 to 75, 66 ideal; Butterick: 15 to 25px, leading 120 to 145 percent). Sans only for figures, tables, captions and the kicker.
- **Four type sizes. Hierarchy by space and italic, not caps and weight** (Butterick; Tufte CSS). Part headings at 1.5 times body with a hairline under them and air above (Distill). No mono capitals in the body.
- **A margin column** at 1100px and up for the crux question and notes (Tufte CSS, Distill's gutter). A crux first in every part (OSTEP). Two to four notes per part at most.
- **Chrome in greys, colour inside the figures.** Cite dots grey when supported, amber or red only when the audit found something. Links underlined in the hairline colour.
- **Figures step out of the text column in named widths**: body, wide, page (Distill's layout ladder). Captions below, 14.5px sans, a bold run-in that states the finding, then what to see, then the source as attribution.
- **No paragraph over 80 words. Print is first class.** Figures and tables do not break across pages. Notes fall inline in print.

## Writing standard

Modified ASD-STE100, as brief.md states it. Complete sentences, twenty words, simple tenses, active voice, one name for one thing, every name explained once, no idioms. The chapter is longer than a brief, so the rule bites harder: a reader who stops after any sentence must know who did what.

## What stays out

The research method, the agent count, the source tiers, the order of the work. They belong in the research archive, which the kicker links in one line.

## The commands

```
chapter-new <slug> --session=<id> --series="Name" --chapter=N [--brand=NAME]
chapter-audit <draft.html> [--fetch] [--verify=<model>]
hq-page publish <slug> --session=<id>
```

`chapter-new` writes the draft with the house tokens. `chapter-audit` checks the structure, fetches each source, checks each quote, asks the verifier model for support, and writes the audit table into the page. `hq-page publish` moves the draft to its address and opens it.
