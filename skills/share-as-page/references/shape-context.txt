THE SHAPE OF THE PAGE. Three levels, far apart. (1) A headline of three to six words at seven times body size, the message itself ("Two chips moved."); a lede whose first sentence, in bold, is the one-sentence claim with a verb; then a dark block saying what needs them, or that nothing does. (2) One row per claim, a full sentence each, with a status dot and one figure: reading only the rows gives the argument. (3) Under each claim, never collapsed, the artifact: the screenshot of the real UI, the diff hunk, the raw rows, the literal prompt, one per claim. Prose ABOUT the evidence is not evidence. Draw only when the reader must trace a branch, merge or loop, with `hq-diagram`, which runs the draw test; one sentence stays a sentence, twenty numbers or fewer a table, a straight sequence a list. Every number names the row or log line it came from. Start with `hq-page new <slug> --session={agent}` (add --brand=NAME when the page is about a brand): it writes a DRAFT (your hub's _drafts/ folder, which nothing publishes) with your id, kicker and tokens filled in, and prints the path. Fill it there, then run `hq-page publish <slug> --session={agent}`, which moves it to its address and opens it in the Hub window. The template is {template}; read {brief} first. THE PAGE WEARS THE BRAND IT IS ABOUT, decided once per session from the conversation: `hq-theme {agent} --brand=NAME` binds it, and every later `hq-theme {agent}` keeps it; a page about nobody wears the house. If the header says NO THEME ON RECORD, do the quick research yourself (the Kopi brand record via set_active_brand, or the site) and record it with `hq-theme --learn=NAME --accent=#hex --from='the source'`; tell the user in one line what you recorded or that the brand declares nothing and stays on the house; do not ask permission. No paragraph over 80 words; a decision that exists only in prose is a red flag. Artifacts are not boxes: a quote gets a left rule, code gets the light neutral panel, a screenshot gets a hairline, is cropped to the passage and never drawn on, links to the live thing it shows, and has a sentence caption below; the kicker is two items, brand and date. SHOW IT, DO NOT DESCRIBE IT. A claim about a UI carries a screenshot of that UI, taken this session, before and after when something changed. A claim about data carries the rows. A claim about code carries the hunk. A login is not a reason to skip it: for Kopi, promotions/scripts/render-authed.ts signs in as a real user and screenshots any trykopi.ai path; for a local page, headless Chrome --screenshot. The only honest gap is a before that nobody captured at the time, and the page says so where the image would sit. Look at every screenshot before you embed it.
