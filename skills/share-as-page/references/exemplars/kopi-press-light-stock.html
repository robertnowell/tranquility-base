Proposal

Stop operating the newsletter workflow

We ran a standard product-launch newsletter by hand today. Kopi already owns about two thirds of it — but one of the pieces it owns is actively working against the operator.

Read against origin/main @ 8510d3268 (Aug 8, 2026) · worked example: TW 48542237, U Vape STLTH Loop Max pods

What we actually did by hand

A newsletter task landed in Teamwork. Nine steps later there were two drafts. Of those nine, Kopi performed three unaided; four it could perform with plumbing it mostly already has; one is a defect where Kopi's own pipeline produced the error; and one is a gap nothing covers. That distribution is the argument — this is not a request for a new product surface, it is a request to connect parts that already exist and to stop one of them from misfiring.

1. The testimonial defect — two subsystems in direct conflict

This is the one to fix first, because it is not a missing feature. It is Kopi proposing an illegal element and then relying on the operator to veto it.

Canada's Tobacco and Vaping Products Act bans testimonials and endorsements for vaping products (s.30.21). Today's second draft came back with a five-star customer-quote card, named reviewer and all. Tracing it:

  • features/accuracy/citations/scoped-registry.ts — fetchGroundingQuotes() pulls customer quotes into the brief-scoped registry on every generation. It takes brandId, brief and limit. There is no policy parameter and no caller-side gate.
  • features/customer-voices/grounding.ts — formatAvailableQuotes() renders them as "Real customer quotes available (OPTIONAL): feature ONE as a testimonial", dropped into both the plan prompt and the body prompt.
  • features/email-templates/email-plan.ts — once the planner bites, the plan carries a mandate: "Feature this real customer testimonial in this section, word-for-word." By then it is a committed section, not a suggestion.

Meanwhile lib/brand/brand-instructions-prompt.ts is a genuinely well-built stage-scoped enforcement layer — its own doc comment says constraints "must bind where decisions are made: a downstream MUST-follow can't veto a topic an upstream stage already committed to," citing the U Vape cessation-ideas bug from July. The lesson was learned and written down. The voice-grounding path simply predates it and bypasses it.

One subsystem inserts the prohibited element into the prompt; another is expected to talk the model out of it. Even a perfectly worded brand rule would be resolving a contradiction the pipeline manufactured.Why the prompt-only fix isn't a fix

Fix: gate the retrieval, not the prompt. buildBriefScopedRegistry gains a voice switch driven by brand policy, so for a testimonial-prohibited brand the block is never assembled and the planner never sees it. Small, self-contained, testable, and it closes a live compliance exposure on a paying account.

2. Brand rules are prose; some of them are law

generationInstructions is a single free-text blob. It is well-scoped per pipeline stage and it works — U Vape's Canadian spelling, all-caps buttons, real-prices and no-cessation rules all held today. But it is unstructured, unverified, and only as complete as whoever last typed into it. U Vape's blob says nothing about testimonials, because nobody thought to write it down.

Proposal: a small typed brandPolicy beside the prose — a handful of enums, not a rules engine:

testimonials:   prohibited | allowed
urgencyClaims:  brief-only | allowed
healthClaims:   prohibited | allowed
channels:       [email]           // never SMS for vape
priceDisplay:   required | optional

Two properties make this worth more than more prose. First, typed fields gate retrieval — they can switch a subsystem off, which a sentence in a prompt can never do. Second, they become post-generation assertions: after render, check the document and fail the draft rather than hoping a reviewer catches it. Prose keeps everything that isn't mechanical.

The deeper point: a policy set once at the brand level cannot be forgotten by whoever writes today's prompt. That is precisely how it was lost today — I wrote the prompt, and I did not think of it either.

3. Exemplar selection — mostly built, wired to the wrong path

Choosing which past send to model was the most judgement-heavy thing we did, and Kopi already has the concept.

  • features/ideas/utils/resolve-inspired-by-campaign.ts resolves the past campaign an idea is modelled on into a metrics snapshot — rpr, open rate, click rate, revenue, subject, sent-at. Kopi already thinks in "this is based on that winner."
  • create_email already accepts referenceKlaviyoCampaignUrl and distills a sent campaign's structure.

What is missing is the selection rule and the wire between the two. Today's rule, which held up: two windows (same month last year, plus a recent two-month window), rank on revenue per recipient rather than revenue, then filter to the same email class.

Proposal: suggest_reference_campaign(brandId, brief) returning ranked candidates with metrics and a stated reason per candidate, plus referenceStrategy: "auto" on create_email. The operator still overrides — but the default stops being nothing.

One caveat learned today, worth encoding: ranking alone picks the wrong exemplar. The top-scoring recent send was a volume-discount promo; the right reference was a lower-scoring six-flavour drop, because the job was a multi-variant flavour launch. Rank, then pick the structural twin. If the tool ranks and a human picks, that is a good division of labour — but the tool must show its reasons, or the human can't.

4. The send slot is recommended, but never learned

features/ideas/scheduling/brand-schedule.ts assigns each idea "the next open slot on the brand's calendar" from agentConfig.defaultSendDows — defaulting to Tue/Thu — and defaultSendTimePt, defaulting to 10:00 Pacific. It answers which day is free. It does not answer which slot earns.

For U Vape that default lands at 14:00 Atlantic. Its six best product launches of 2026 all went between 17:00 and 18:15 Atlantic. The gap is not small, and the brand's own data has been sitting in the Klaviyo integration the whole time.

A typed-in-Pacific field for a brand in Atlantic Canada

defaultSendTimePt bakes a timezone into the field name. U Vape's stores are in Nova Scotia, which is UTC−3 in summer and UTC−4 in winter — two conversions away from the stored value, in a field that cannot express either. Worth making timezone-aware while the scheduling code is open.

Proposal: derive each brand's default slot from its own send history — median revenue per recipient by weekday × local hour, with launches weighted, and small cells discarded as noise. Store it on the agent config; keep calendar-emptiness as the tiebreak, not the primary signal.

5. Two smaller gaps worth closing in passing

Product pre-flight

feature: {kind:'products'} already fails closed when a ref doesn't resolve, which is the right instinct. But nothing checks whether a featured product is in stock, whether the set has a price spread that makes "all $24.99" a lie, or whether the brief's claimed launch date matches the store's published date. All three are cheap reads against the same catalogue data the pipeline already holds, and all three are the kind of error that ships.

Audience is not compatibility

The first draft told Loop and Loop 2 owners that the new pods "drop straight in." They are exclusively compatible with the Loop Max. The brief said both things — segment on any Loop purchaser, requires the Loop Max device — and nothing in the plan schema keeps those two facts apart, so the model collapsed them.

This is a pipeline-shaped failure, not a model slip: there was no field for compatibility, so there was nothing to validate against. A requires / compatibility field on the brief, treated by the citation validator as a claim needing product-data backing like any other, would have caught it.

What "automatic" looks like

Task lands → brief parsed into typed fields → reference campaign auto-selected and shown with reasons → products pre-flighted against the store → two drafts generated under brand-policy gates → policy assertions run against the rendered document → send slot recommended from the brand's own history → links posted back to the card. A human reviews two finished drafts and picks one.

Most of that rail already exists. content_calendar_entries → agent week → generated drafts is live and running the Mirai cascade weekly. U Vape's newsletters could ride the same rail. The two things it lacks are an intake — a Teamwork card is not a calendar entry — and the policy layer.

How solid this is

Everything above is a read of origin/main at 8510d3268 from a read-only checkout — I did not run any of it. The testimonial trace is three files deep and I am confident in it, but it deserves ten minutes of confirmation against a live generation before it becomes a PR description. The claim that most of the automation rail already exists is inferred from the calendar and agent-week services being live, not from tracing an end-to-end run.

Small on its own, and the section was being deleted anyway, but it suggests component defaults are not uniformly brand-resolved.
