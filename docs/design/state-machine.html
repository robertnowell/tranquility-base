Tranquility Base — input state machine

Every state, every input, and what actually happens today. Read from the handlers rather than from memory. Rows shaded red are gaps: behaviour that is undefined, inconsistent, or wrong. There are seven of them.

Inputs. ⌃⌥ tap = hear next · ⌥ hold = reply · ⇧ tap = pause/resume · Esc = dismiss · plus the panel buttons. A modifier gesture is cancelled if any other key or click happens during it.

The seven gaps, ranked

  1. Escape is dead in three states (idle, listening, settings) because isBusyOnScreen tests isRecording — the button flag — and not isListening, the flag the hold gesture actually sets. So Escape does nothing during the single most common interaction. One condition, three states fixed.
  2. ⌃⌥ while the mic is open starts an announcement into a live microphone. It would literally record itself. Should be ignored while recording.
  3. ⌥ with no target (idle, or during Preparing) records and transcribes a reply that has nowhere to go, then fails. Refuse at the gesture instead of spending a transcription to discover it.
  4. ⌃⌥ during the send window is genuinely ambiguous: the reply still sends while a new summary starts playing. Needs a decision, not a fix.
  5. ⌃⌥ while paused leaves the panel reading Paused until the next announcement renders. Cosmetic, one line.
  6. Escape during transcription has no state to hang off. Lowest value: the window is about a second.
  7. ⇧ during Preparing does nothing. Arguably it should cancel, since "stop this" is what the key means everywhere else.

The pattern behind most of these: the panel tracks isRecording, isListening, isSpeakingNow, awaitingConfirm and isIdle as five independent booleans, and every guard picks its own subset. That is why Escape works in some states and not others, and why isBusyOnScreen could silently omit the hold path. One enum with one case per state would make each of these unrepresentable rather than merely fixed.
